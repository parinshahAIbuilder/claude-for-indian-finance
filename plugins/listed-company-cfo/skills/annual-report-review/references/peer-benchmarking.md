# Benchmarking against a peer annual report

Used when the client supplies a competitor's published annual report, or names
one. The purpose is disclosure practice, not numbers. Do not compare margins or
growth: that is equity research and it is not what a reporting review is for.

---

## Choosing the comparison

The useful peer is one that is listed, reports under the same framework, has the
same financial year end, and runs the same business model. A peer in the same
sector with a different model will mislead on exactly the point that matters.

Where no peer is supplied and the question is "what should we be disclosing",
a recently listed company in the same sector is usually the best source, because
the disclosure will have been through an issue process.

---

## Method

1. **Extract the peer's note index**, not the whole document. The headings tell
   you the shape of the disclosure.
2. **Line the two indexes up.** Note which topics the peer carries that the
   client does not, and which the client carries that the peer does not. The
   second direction occasionally finds a disclosure the client makes
   unnecessarily.
3. **Read the peer's version of each note the client lacks.** Extract the actual
   format: the column headings, the bands, the derivation.
4. **Ask what the difference is evidence of.** Three possibilities, and they are
   not equally interesting:
   - The peer discloses more of the same thing. Useful, low value.
   - The peer discloses something the client omits entirely. Useful, and the
     format is now in hand.
   - **The peer treats the same transaction differently.** This is the finding
     worth having, and no disclosure checklist will produce it.

---

## What this method found on a live review

The client is a managed workspace operator. The peer is a listed managed
workspace operator reporting for the same year.

**Revenue classification.** The peer splits its revenue between Ind AS 116 lease
income and Ind AS 115 revenue from contracts with customers, reporting core
membership income as **operating lease income** on the basis that the arrangement
conveys the right to control an identified space. Only service, ancillary and fit
out revenue sits under Ind AS 115. The client reported the whole of its
equivalent revenue under Ind AS 115 with no split at all.

That reaches the face of the profit and loss, the revenue note and the lessor
note together. It is a classification question, and the right output is not a
finding but a request that the company form and disclose a view, because an
investor comparing the two companies will ask.

**Lessor disclosure.** The peer's lease note opens with "Company as a lessor" and
gives a six band maturity table of future minimum lease rentals receivable. The
client's note was headed "As Lessee" only, with no lessor section.

**Lease options as lessee.** The peer discloses undiscounted future rental
payments split between cancellable and non cancellable options. The client
disclosed neither.

**Ratios.** The peer discloses **two** debt equity ratios, one including lease
liabilities and one excluding, with the numerator and denominator described in
their own columns. The client disclosed one, excluding lease liabilities, without
saying so. For a business whose lease liabilities exceed its borrowings, the
single ratio understates leverage to a reader, and the peer's approach is the
answer.

**Struck off companies and Rule 11(e).** The peer tabulates every counterparty by
name, nature of balance, amount and relationship, and gives full intermediary
details including dates and ultimate beneficiary. The client gave single sentence
negative statements.

**Candour.** The peer disclosed in its notes that daily backups of its books were
not held on servers in India for a period, and described the remediation. Most
companies would leave that in the audit report if anywhere. Worth putting in
front of a client who has an adverse CARO remark of their own and has not
mentioned it in the notes.

---

## Writing it up

Give the peer's figure and the client's side by side, in the peer's own units,
and say which note each comes from. Do not convert: a reader who wants to check
will go to the peer's annual report.

Be explicit that the peer's treatment is evidence of accepted practice, not of
the only permissible treatment. The output is a question the client must answer,
not an instruction.

Leave out anything where the client's disclosure is already equivalent. A
benchmark report that lists twenty differences, fifteen of which are cosmetic,
buries the three that matter.
