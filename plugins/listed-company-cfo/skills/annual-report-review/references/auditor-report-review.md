# Reviewing the auditor's report, CARO annexure and IFC report

Read these against the financial statements, never on their own. The highest
yield findings are contradictions between the two documents, and between the
standalone and consolidated reports signed on the same day by the same partner.

---

## A. The opinion and the body

| Check | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Opinion covers all four statements plus notes | A statement omitted from the list |
| Period described as the year ended, not the period ended | |
| Consolidated opinion refers to the Group and its associates, not "the Company", and says consolidated before state of affairs, profit, cash flows and changes in equity | Routinely copied from the standalone |
| Framework cited as Indian Accounting Standards under section 133 read with the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules 2015 | "Accounting Standards" is Division I language and contradicts the opinion paragraph |
| Basis for opinion names the ICAI Code of Ethics and asserts sufficiency | |
| Key audit matters are those of most significance, SA 701 para 9 | One KAM in an IPO year invites the question of what else was considered. Revenue recognition, share issue expense accounting and deferred tax recoverability against accumulated losses are the usual candidates |
| KAM response text actually describes the matter it sits under | A leases KAM whose procedures open "our audit procedures in respect of evaluation of receivables" is a copy paste survivor |
| KAM note references exist | A KAM citing a material accounting policies note that is absent from the statements is conclusive proof the note is missing |
| SA 720 paragraph names the right statements | The consolidated report referring to the standalone financial statements |
| Going concern, material uncertainty section where required | |
| Emphasis of matter and other matter where warranted | |

## B. Section 143(3) clauses

Walk the list. Clauses are frequently absent rather than wrong.

| Clause | Subject |
|---|---|
| (a) | Information and explanations obtained |
| (b) | Proper books of account |
| (c) | **Reports on the accounts of branch offices under section 143(8)** |
| (d) | Statements in agreement with the books |
| (e) | Compliance with the accounting standards |
| (f) | **Observations having an adverse effect on the functioning of the company** |
| (g) | Director disqualification under section 164(2) |
| (h) | **Qualification or adverse remark on maintenance of accounts** |
| (i) | Internal financial controls, with a separate annexure |
| (j) | Other matters under Rule 11 |

Clauses (c), (f) and (h) are the usual omissions. Clause (c) matters wherever
the accounting records show branches.

## C. Rule 11

| Sub rule | Subject | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Pending litigation impact | Note reference |
| (b) | Long term contracts including derivatives | |
| (c) | Transfer to the Investor Education and Protection Fund | |
| (e) | Intermediaries and funding parties representations | Two note references, both frequently off by one |
| (f) | Dividend and section 123 compliance | The sentence "had not proposed any final dividend in the previous year, which was declared and paid during the year" is self contradictory and appears in many reports |
| (g) | Audit trail | Whether it operated throughout the year, whether tampered with, whether preserved, **and whether enabled at database level** |

On (g), compare the auditor's wording with the company's own note. Where the
note is qualified, "to the extent it was enabled and recorded in the respective
years", and the auditor's clause is unqualified, the auditor is more definite
than management. Raise it.

## D. CARO 2020, all twenty one clauses

Confirm every clause is addressed, including those reported as not applicable.

Clause by clause points that recur:

- **(i)(c), (i)(d), (ix)(b)** establish title deeds, revaluation and wilful
  defaulter status. Where the financial statements omit the corresponding
  Schedule III disclosure, the evidence already exists. Cite it.
- **(ii)(b)** quarterly or monthly returns filed with banks against the books.
  Where they do not agree, the clause requires details. A bare statement that
  they do not agree, with no amount, quarter or nature, is incomplete reporting.
  It should also be matched by a disclosure in the financial statements.
- **(iii)(a)** loans and guarantee table. Reconcile the outstanding figures to the
  loans notes and the contingent liability note. A split between related parties
  and others here that contradicts notes describing the whole balance as related
  party lending is a direct conflict.
- **(iii)(c)** against **(iii)(f)**. A clause stating that repayment schedules
  have been stipulated contradicts a clause reporting amounts repayable on
  demand.
- **(iii)(f)** table columns. "All parties" is the total column and cannot be nil
  while "related parties" carries a figure.
- **(iv)** sections 185 and 186. A clause denying any guarantee contradicts a
  guarantee reported at (iii)(a). Note that the section 186(11) exemption covers a
  wholly owned subsidiary company and not a limited liability partnership.
- **(vii)(b)** statutory dues. Requires the amount deposited under protest as a
  separate column. Totals should agree to the contingent liability note. Where
  the heading names income tax but only GST rows appear, narrow the heading.
- **(x)(a)** IPO utilisation, with a note reference. **(x)(b)** preferential
  allotment, which should not read as inconsistent with a subsequent events note
  disclosing a board approved preferential issue.
- **(xx)** CSR. This clause usually states the section 135 test correctly, as net
  profit of five crore rupees or more in the immediately preceding financial
  year. Where the financial statements state it as a three year average, the
  auditor's own wording is the correction to adopt.
- **(xxi)** consolidated only. Qualifications and adverse remarks in the CARO
  reports of companies included in the consolidated financial statements, with
  names, CIN and clause numbers.

**The contradiction to look for first.** Where the standalone CARO contains an
adverse remark and the consolidated clause (xxi) reports that there are none,
the two reports contradict each other. The holding company is a company included
in the consolidated financial statements. Both are signed on the same date by the
same partner, and the point is objectively verifiable.

A clause (xxi) table naming an associate with an adverse remark is also a
finding about the financial statements, not only about the audit report. An
associate reported for cash losses, clause (xvii), against a material investment
and loan exposure, is an impairment indicator under Ind AS 28 and Ind AS 36 that
the notes should address.

## E. Internal financial controls report

- Scope stated consistently in the section 143(3)(i) clause, in the annexure
  heading and in the opinion paragraph. Three different scopes in three places is
  common in consolidated reports, where the associates are included at the top
  and dropped by the opinion.
- Clause cited as (i), not the digit 1.
- The phrase "essential components of internal control" is complete.
- Cross references to an "other matters paragraph below" actually point at one.
- **The opinion itself.** An unmodified opinion that controls operated effectively
  sits poorly beside an adverse CARO remark on bank returns, a balance sheet that
  does not balance, and an EPS computed on the wrong base. Raise it as a question
  for the auditor rather than as an assertion.

## F. The note cross reference map

Build this table every time. It is the single most useful page to hand the
auditor.

| Report | Clause | Cites | Actual note | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Check every reference in both reports: KAM note references, Rule 11(a), 11(e)
twice, 11(g), CARO (ii)(b) and (x)(a).

Where several are wrong by the same offset, the cause is renumbering, not
carelessness. Say so, and establish **which numbering each document was prepared
against** before proposing corrections. The auditor may have worked from the
current set while the printed statements used the old one, in which case the
report is right and the statements are wrong.

---

## G. Document hygiene

Check the file, not only the text.

- Unresolved review comments left in the document.
- Tracked changes not accepted.
- Metadata: author, last modified by, last modified date, revision count. A
  report dated 19 May that was last modified on 14 August, by a firm other than
  the signing firm, is a fact the Audit Committee should know.
- UDIN present, and different for the standalone and consolidated reports.
- Place and date, and consistency with the financial statements.

Never circulate the Word file itself. Metadata and comments travel with it.
