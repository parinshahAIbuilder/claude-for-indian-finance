# Report format and house style

---

## House style

**No hyphen or dash characters in prose.** Standing client preference, absolute.
Write "non current", "cross reference", "pre signing", "year end", "tie out".
Rephrase where the compound becomes awkward: "second pass" rather than
"re review". This applies to the report, to any letter, and to annotation
comments embedded in marked up documents.

Figures in lakhs to two decimals with thousands separators, matching the
statements. Quote the figure exactly as printed when citing it, then give your
recomputation alongside.

Every finding carries its location: page, note number, and line description.
A finding the reader cannot find is not actionable.

Name the arithmetic. "Components add to 13,059.2753, which rounds to 13,059.28
against a printed 13,059.27" beats "casting error" every time.

---

## Severity rubric

Grade on consequence to the reader, not on the size of the number.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Stops publication. A face statement that does not balance or foot, a headline figure that is wrong, two documents stating different values for one figure, a mandatory disclosure wholly absent, or an internal contradiction a reader will see unaided |
| **High** | Correct before circulation. Material disclosure gap, accounting treatment that needs resolving, inconsistency between standalone and consolidated |
| **Medium** | Disclosure, classification, cross reference or presentation point that should be corrected |
| **Low** | Formatting, wording, typography |

Four genuine Critical findings carry far more weight than twelve inflated ones.
If everything is critical, nothing is.

## Report structure

**A. Executive conclusion.** One of three, stated plainly, with two or three
sentences of reasoning:

- Ready for finalisation
- Ready subject to minor corrections
- Not ready, material issues remain

**B. Closure status of previous observations.** Only on a second or later pass.
The matrix below.

**C. Critical findings.** Each with: the issue, the exact figure as printed, page
and note reference, why it is an issue, the correct treatment, and the
recommended action.

**D. Detailed review**, by area: balance sheet, profit and loss, cash flow,
notes, standalone against consolidated, Ind AS, related parties, borrowings and
finance costs, tax, EPS, disclosures, presentation. Where an area tests clean,
write "no exception identified after review" rather than omitting it.

**E. Reconciliation exceptions.** One table, every mismatch found:

| Area | Figure A | Figure B | Difference | Location | Reason | Action |

**F. Auditor query list.** The questions a senior statutory auditor or Audit
Committee member will ask on reading these statements. Written as questions, not
as findings.

**G. Sign off checklist.** Ticked, with the failures visible.

**Scope note.** Files read with their modification dates, what was recomputed,
and what could not be verified from the documents supplied and why.

---

## The closure matrix

The deliverable that makes a repeat pass worth more than the first.

| Previous observation | Required change | Change made? | Page or note | Verification performed | Status | Further action |

Status is one of:

- **Closed.** Verified by recomputation, not by reading the wording.
- **Partial.** Some years or some documents corrected.
- **Open.** Not incorporated.
- **Open, new issue created.** Incorporated, and the fix broke something else.
  Always list these immediately after the Critical findings, because nobody else
  is looking for them.
- **Withdrawn.** The original observation was wrong, or has been overtaken.
  Say so plainly and explain why.

Close with a count: how many of how many are closed. It is the number the Audit
Committee will ask for.

---

## Standard of proof in the writing

Distinguish, in the finding itself, between:

- A confirmed error, where the arithmetic on the page is wrong.
- A point requiring management or auditor confirmation, where the answer depends
  on facts outside the documents.

Where you are inferring, say so and say what would settle it. A finding that
reads "this is consistent with minimum alternate tax under section 115JB, which
would mean unrecognised MAT credit of about 107.50 lakhs, please confirm with the
tax team" is useful and safe. The same point asserted as fact is neither.

## Deliverables beyond the report

**Marked up documents.** Frequently requested and often more useful than the
report, because the finding sits on the figure. Use the `review-markup` skill.
Colour code by severity and put the full finding in the comment, so the document
is self contained.

**Draft disclosures.** When asked for the missing notes rather than a list of
gaps, draft them with the company's own figures wherever derivable, and mark
everything else as requiring company input. Never invent a figure to complete a
template. Say which cells are supported and which are placeholders, and say it in
the file itself, not only in the covering message.

**Auditor correspondence.** A different document from the report. Open by
recording what tested correctly. One numbered paragraph per issue, each with a
specific request. Separate the points that are drafting corrections from those
that need the auditor's judgement, because the first set can be accepted in bulk
and the second cannot.

**Audit Committee pack.** The closure matrix, the Critical findings, and the
query list. Nothing else.

---

## What not to do

Never recommend that the statements be signed or not signed. Report the position
and let the Board decide, and say so explicitly at the end.

Never circulate a Word file carrying metadata or unresolved comments.

Never present a draft disclosure with unfilled placeholders as though it were
finished.
